## What it does

There's a presence sensor hidden in there, and most people don't notice it. When you walk past, it quickens and churns. When you stop, it settles, and the longer you stay the more it changes. The clouds slow down, starlings gather, and then there's a sunset. Stay with the sea grass and the tide comes in over it. In the waves a whirl opens and starts turning, and much later a point of light rises in the middle.

It also keeps track of which patterns hold people still, and plays those more.

Phones steal your attention. The AI behind Instagram is trained to keep you there. Estoa is like a stillness-rewarding social media feed. It focuses your attention on one thing, and it brings you into the room you're in.

## What you see

A disc of light behind frosted glass, about 30 cm across. I wanted really dreamy diffusion, almost so you don't notice there are diodes. There's a gap around the disc, because it's really cool when you look at it from the side. Some light leaks out around the back onto the wall as a soft halo. That wasn't planned, and I liked it more than I expected.

The patterns are generated live and never repeat.

## How I made it

It started on September 25 with an LED disc and an ESP32 on my desk. The first thing it did was default to a rainbow. I hate that.

I picked up a round mirror at a flea market, and for a while the plan was to mount the disc on it and let it rotate. On October 2 I dropped that. Presence was the more interesting idea, and rotation was more that could break. When the radar first worked on the bench I thought, how does it know presence? It's pretty awesome.

The enclosure is 3D printed, and I printed, sanded, soldered and assembled all of it by hand. The soldering took me all freaking day. The clear wall is printed in four quarters, because that's what fits on the printer, then sanded and glued with epoxy. Straight off the printer every LED turned into a vertical streak, and sanding fixed it. I'd never glued with epoxy before, and the first joint came apart when I picked it up too soon.

The box on the back took three tries. It needed to be mostly closed, because there's a lot of dust in Mexico. On the second one the posts bulged when I melted the brass inserts in. On the third the white filament ran out, so I finished it in clear. It looks cool, actually.

The piece was meant to stand on the mirror. The drill went through the steel pan fine, then stalled on the glass. I pushed, and it cracked as it went through. I should have been more patient. So it went up without the mirror, clamped straight to the back box, and the halo on the wall is what you get instead.

## Parts

- 255 LEDs in concentric rings on a 242 mm disk
- ESP32-S3, patterns generated live
- LD2410C 24 GHz presence radar, hidden under the glass
- Clear PLA wall and spider, frosted glass, printed back box
- Battery inside, one switch, Wi-Fi updates
- About 30 cm across, 10.5 cm off the wall
