## How it got here

### From a spinning mirror to a sensor

It started on September 25 as an LED disk and an ESP32 on my desk, and a lot of generative patterns. A few days later I found a round mirror at a flea market in Mexico City that used to turn on a table, and for a while the plan was to make the whole thing rotate.

On October 2 I dropped that. Rotation was more that could break, and I wanted to work out what the piece was actually about. Presence seemed more interesting, so I got a radar that can tell when someone's there.

{/* PLACEHOLDER (Pangram flagged, Dan to rewrite): the stillness moment while testing at home; the show patterns rebuilt with three states: nobody, walking past, holding still */}

The feed came last, the morning it got its name.

### The enclosure

The enclosure is 3D printed. A Claude agent modelled it in OpenSCAD from my measurements, sketches and ideas, and I printed, soldered, drilled and put it all together by hand. The wall around the disk is clear PLA, printed in four quarters and glued with clear epoxy. Straight off the printer the layer lines smeared every LED into a vertical streak, so I wet-sanded it, and that turned it into an even frost.

I printed about a dozen small test pieces before any real part. Fit tests, insert tests, and stacks of "height bricks" to find where the glass should sit. With the glass about 5.5 cm above the LEDs, no single LED shows through.

The box on the back took three tries. The first left the battery's end open to dust. The second was sealed, but the battery bay was half a millimetre too tight, the posts bulged when I melted the brass inserts in and two inserts pulled out, and the ESP32's USB port ran into the end of its plug tunnel. A one-hour test print checked the fixes, and the third box worked. The white filament ran out partway through printing it, so I finished it in clear, and now it fades from white to clear. I like it.

The board slides into a groove in the box wall. There was no way to get a screwdriver to the screws on that end, so the groove does their job.

### The mirror

The piece was designed to stand on the mirror from the flea market, which is glass in a black steel pan. The disk was going to bolt through it. The steel drilled easily. The glass didn't seem to be going anywhere, so I leaned on the drill, and it cracked from the hole straight across to the rim.

So it went up without the mirror. The disk and its wall clamp straight onto the back box, and some light leaks out around the edge of the box onto the wall as a soft halo. I liked that more than I expected, and kept it.

### The patterns

{/* PLACEHOLDER (Pangram flagged, Dan to rewrite): patterns generated live on the ESP32; Dan directed them, Claude agents wrote the code, sent back with notes */}

I tuned them in a browser preview that runs the same code and shows the piece in 3D, with buttons for nobody, moving and still, and in a simulator that runs the real firmware on my laptop and renders a visit as a GIF, like the ones above. Once it's on the wall the USB port is hard to get to, so new patterns go onto the board over Wi-Fi. It takes about a minute.
