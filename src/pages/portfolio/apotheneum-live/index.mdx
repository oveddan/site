> ❯ I have a midi twister fighter. Do you have access to the API repository that we have? I want to figure out how to turn the bottom right knob. When you press it, the trigger does a tap tempo. — Aug 16, 9:04 pm

> ❯ check the transcript of my call with FOSS, from wispr. particular on the visual feedback mentions. extract what you can about it, cause I want to encode that feedback and incorporate it into the set — Aug 18, 9:46 pm

> ❯ You just delete the old swatches, and maybe we don't need three colors. Maybe we just need two. Why do we need three colors? — Aug 19

Excerpts from what came out of it three days later, a standing design file in the show repo, `design/color-system.md`. The quoted words are from the call:

> **Avoid primary vertices, not hues.** Pure red, green, blue, yellow, white — "just really not very good for LEDs." The rule is distance from the vertex, not which hue: his own favorite hue is blue, and pure blue is his named example of what to avoid, while deep blue + cyan is the single frame he called "beautiful."
>
> **Depth comes from brightness, not desaturation.** On LEDs, dropping saturation mixes in white and reads as washed out rather than deep. Keep field colors at saturation ≥ 85.
>
> **No white.** "Underwhelming and cold… feels like a sound effect more than the actual show."
>
> **Composition = one hue-family field + one contrasting accent**, the accent on fixed geometry and at a lower presence than the field. Two bright fields side by side is the explicit "terrible" failure, even when neither color is individually wrong.
>
> **Green is not accent-only.** A cold, glacial teal/mint reads as a valid full field color. Saturated, grass-primary green is what's actually rejected.

Four days later the rules became code: [PR #126](https://github.com/Apotheneum/Apotheneum/pull/126), a base class with a primary and a secondary color role, so the patterns written after it draw from one global palette.
